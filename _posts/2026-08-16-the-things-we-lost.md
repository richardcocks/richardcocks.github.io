# The things we lost

For all the promises of the internet to keep information, parts of it just quietly disappear without trace.

## PC Gamer (UK)

I grew up reading PC Gamer, I didn't subscribe, but it was an occassional treat, picked up at WHSmith when pocket money allowed for it. 
I'd skip the previews to read the reviews, they were what I cared about most. Forget what might be good in the future, I wanted to know what's good right now. The real prize however was the cover disc.

A CD full of treasures, including things such as full copies of software or indie games, or more often, demo versions of games.  I spent countless hours on the first levels of various games, mastering their demo editions without ever purchasing the full game.

But there was a hidden treasure on every CD.

## The Reviews Database

Hidden on each CD ( and later DVD ) was a review database, a browseable log of every game PC Gamer had ever reviewed.

For someone who only bought the occassional copy of the magazine, this was the real treasure trove. A chance to catch up on all the reviews I'd missed. In my memory, it also included the review text, but the internet archive corrects that memory to only being a selected few reviews, with most only carrying the score and price and whether it was "Game of the Month".

## A disappearing act

So I recently, overcome with nostalgia, but more importantly a chance to prove someone wrong, I acquired a copy of the last ever DVD, to extract the review database and analyze it.

First off, I plotted "Game of the Month" scores over time, and prices of those games over time, including adjusting for inflation. That's almost worthy of it's own blog post, but the spoiler is that the most expensive "Game of the Month" of all time was "Wing Commander II: Heart of the Tiger CD" from 1994 clocking in at an inflation adjusted £147.92.

However, the "Game of the Month" ended before PC Gamer did, and so I had to extend the dataset. I picked the top scoring review of each month to substitute. This made the dataset a lot noisier, but it also revealed some flaws.

Sticking out like a saw thumb was PCGamer 139, "Live for speed". Firstly I'd never heard of this game, secondly it seemed to be the highest scoring review of the issue with a 81% score.

As a sidebar, I should explain that PC Gamer UK reviews a lot lower than PC Gamer US. PC Gamer UK had never scored above 96% in its entire run since 1993 until Baldurs Gate 3 scored 97%.

Half-life, Quake 2, and a handful of other titles shared 96%, but there was always scope for games to get better.

PC Gamer US by contrast already had 98% scores early in its run.

Anyway, here was "Live for Speed" sitting on 81% as an apparent top score of the month. So I queried which other games were reviewed that month, apparently just no other games were.

This led me on a treasure hunt, the internet archives PCGamer cover discs, so I downloaded the one from 139 ( which should only only have games up to 138, the current issue reviews were not in the database ), 140 and 141.

From 140 onward, it seems that 139 reviews ended up badged as 140, but it's not then not clear what actually happened to what ought to have been issue 140's reviews. Early discs post 140 don't have the "Live for Speed" entry as 139, but instead 140. That singular title seemed to be later corrected, but not its siblings, leaving it as the best reviewed title of 139 according to the database.

## This was never made available online

For all the hopes of the early internet, and that of "web2.0", open information and data freely available to be queried and remixed, what we got instead was data hoarding and private information never made available.

We never got the full PCGamer review database online, and so it lies fossilised, incorrectly, on the last ever DVD issue 211.

## It's hard to even find a physical copy

The internet wanted information to be free, but despite the interconnectedness of most of the world, I can't find a physical copy of Issue 139 and Issue 140 on eBay. I'd love to, just to verify what actually was reviewed in each copy! 

I can't even find scans of the covers, except the "subscriber edition" which doesn't have the text advertising what was reviewed!
